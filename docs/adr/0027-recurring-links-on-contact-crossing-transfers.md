# Transfers crossing an own Wallet and a Contact Wallet may carry the matching-direction recurring link

Recording a Recurring Income whose payer is tracked as a Contact Wallet (e.g. Chiara's 300 €/month) had no way to mark Occurrences Paid: a receipt from a contact is a Transfer — Contact Wallets are never the Wallet of an Income (ADR-0017) — and the typed-link rules never allowed a link on a Transfer (ADR-0010/0011, coded as "Transfer never carries a link"), so a Transfer's Occurrence could never be Paid, and the red "N unpaid" badge grew forever with no workaround (recording a linked Income would double-count the money). The mirror gap existed on the cost side: paying a recurring cost *to* a Contact person would be a Transfer to that Contact.

A Transfer whose legs are exactly **one own (non-Contact) Wallet and one Contact Wallet** may now optionally carry one recurring link — the pair-and-direction rule decided at grilling:

- **The pair.** Exactly one own Wallet and one Contact Wallet. Own↔own and Contact↔Contact Transfers never qualify.
- **The direction picks the kind.** Money in from a Contact Wallet (source = Contact) may link a Recurring Income; money out to a Contact Wallet (destination = Contact) may link a Recurring Cost. The two directions are mutually exclusive, so a Transfer never carries both links.
- **The link mechanics are the typed contract unchanged** (issues #57/#61, ADR-0010/0011/0016): linking pays exactly the oldest Unpaid Occurrence at link time, pinned on the row (`occurrence_date`) and never reassigned by later date edits; the amount is never checked against the definition's; Skipped Occurrences are never payable; unlinking or deleting the Transfer frees the Occurrence; deleting the definition severs (`ON DELETE SET NULL`); a Paid Occurrence can never be Skipped; no effect on Monthly Spendable (Occurrences count by due date whether paid or not).
- **The edit rule.** A write that would leave a link on a no-longer-qualifying Transfer — a leg swap that drops the Contact Wallet, or a direction that no longer matches — is rejected with a rule error naming the problem, never silently severed. The user unlinks in the same form and the edit succeeds. (Transfer legs are immutable on edit today, so the reachable rejections are link-sets on a pair that never qualified or that qualifies the other direction; the check runs against the pair the write leaves in place.)
- **Out of scope, as decided.** No Monthly Spendable change; past months stay as they are (no un-skip or migration campaign); definitions still carry no Wallet or person — the picker offers every definition of the matching kind, user picks by name as with Incomes today; imports and exports never carry links (unchanged).

## Considered Options

- **Record the receipt as an Income on the Contact Wallet** — rejected in ADR-0017 and still rejected: an Income on a contact would raise both the balance and Net Worth with no money movement behind it; money coming in from a contact is always a Transfer.
- **Sever the link when the pair stops qualifying** — rejected: a silent sever would free an Occurrence the user believes Paid; the red badge would grow back without any edit by the user.
- **Allow the link on any Transfer touching a Contact Wallet (Contact↔Contact included)** — rejected: a Transfer between two contacts moves money the user is not a party to (neither leg is the user's own Wallet), so no real recurring receipt or payment of the user's is behind it.
- **Let the amount or the direction be user-chosen** — rejected: the direction of the money through the pair *is* the semantic (repayment received vs. payment made), so the link kind must follow it; the amount stays unchecked like every other link.
- **Retroactively fix past months (auto-un-skip / migrate)** — rejected: going-forward only, like the rest of the link contract; the reporter's backlog is cleared by linking the Transfers already recorded.

## Consequences

- API and rules: `_check_create_rules` and the update path in `app/services/transactions.py` no longer hard-reject links on Transfers; they enforce the pair-and-direction rule, and `create_transaction` stores the link and its pin on qualifying Transfer rows exactly like on Expenses and Incomes. The schema validators keep only the kind-locks an Expense or Income violates (they cannot see Wallet types); the Transfer rule lives in the service.
- Reads needed no change: the paid set (`paid_occurrence_dates`), the definition's ledger filter/jump, and the form's "Pays the occurrence of <date>" helper all follow the link columns, so linked Transfers flow through — verified by tests rather than special-cased.
- Frontend: `TransactionForm` renders the Recurring picker on a Transfer only while the chosen pair qualifies — money-in offers Recurring Incomes, money-out offers Recurring Costs — with the same inline "＋ Add…" sentinel, None = unlink, and occurrence-date helper; a stale draft for a pair that no longer qualifies is sent as null, never rejected.
- The extrapolation "Transfer never carries a link" that ADR-0010/0011's typed-link contract was coded and documented under (module docstrings, CONTEXT.md, schema comments) is superseded for the qualifying pair; the ADRs themselves — tracking-only definitions, hand-recorded payments, occurrence-based Paid state, the typed contract — stand.
