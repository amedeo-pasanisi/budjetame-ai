# Skipped occurrences count for nothing

Sometimes an occurrence of a Recurring Cost or Recurring Income simply does not apply — the user does not have to pay it, or will not receive it. We added a third Occurrence state, Skipped: a per-occurrence user mark that never enters the Backlog, never counts toward Monthly Spendable, and can never be paid by a link — the link walk and the transaction form's picker step over it, and paying one means un-skipping first. Only Unpaid occurrences can be skipped (past or future); un-skipping is always possible; a skip carries no note. The card-level action (Skip/Un-skip button acting on the oldest Unpaid occurrence, clearing a backlog oldest-first) was a separate UI decision.

## Considered Options

- **Skip anchors to the occurrence's period, pins stay date-fixed.** A skip is anchored to the occurrence's period — the month for monthly definitions, the year for yearly, the date itself for daily/weekly — and travels with the occurrence: editing the definition never drops it, and changing the interval unit maps the period along (a skipped month becomes its year, a skipped year becomes its month). A skip whose period holds no Occurrence lies dormant; only un-skipping removes it. Paid pins keep their existing contract (ADR-0010: fixed to the date, never reassigned). The divergence is deliberate: a pin is a historical fact about a Transaction, while a skip is a user intent about "this month's / this year's occurrence" that must survive date edits — rejected the date-fixed anchor for skips because the user expects "I skipped it, so I don't have to pay for this year" to hold after editing the definition.
- **Excise-and-mark, not vanish.** The occurrence stays in the derived timeline as Skipped rather than disappearing — the mark is visible and reversible. (Note: with the card-only UI, visibility is currently limited to the button state; a per-occurrence list was explicitly rejected at grilling.)
- **Skip blocks linking, not auto-un-skip.** A link can never cover a Skipped occurrence — paying is a stronger statement than skipping, so paying one means un-skipping it first. Rejected auto-un-skip-on-link: it would silently erase the user's mark.
- **Per-occurrence skip, not pause/suspend.** A "switch the whole definition off until I flip it back" pause was considered and rejected: different semantics with their own Backlog and Budget rules, and not what was asked for.
