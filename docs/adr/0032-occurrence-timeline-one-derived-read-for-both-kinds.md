# One Occurrence Timeline per definition serves every derived read, for both Recurring kinds

ADR-0028 said a frozen Recurring definition stops counting toward the Budget from its freeze date, and the Budget never did it: a frozen Recurring Cost with an Occurrence still due later in the month kept draining Monthly Spendable. The rule had no home. The pure walkers in `app/recurrence.py` were right, but the facts they need — the pins of the Occurrences a definition's linked Transactions pay, the skips the user excused, and the freeze date — were loaded by every caller in its own way: the dashboard with its own skip query and an `isinstance` dispatch, each Recurring router with three service calls and five queries per definition on the list, the Transaction link pin, the undo path with its own "is this Occurrence already paid" select, and the Occurrences read and skip write copied once per kind. "What counts as Paid, Skipped or generated" was computed in four places, so a rule could be right in the pure module and wrong in production.

We gave the derived reads one home: the **Occurrence Timeline** (`app/services/occurrences.py`, CONTEXT.md). `timeline_for(session, definition)` loads a definition's pins and skips in two queries into a value that answers every question the app asks of its Occurrences — the dates due in a window, the Backlog, the next due date, the Occurrence a new link pays, the Occurrences section's rows, whether a date is Paid, Skipped or an Occurrence at all — and `set_skipped` is the one skip/un-skip write. The Timeline serves Recurring Costs and Recurring Incomes alike: the two kinds differ only in which foreign-key columns their pins and skips hang off, and that is the only kind-specific code in the module. The two Recurring logic modules keep the definition's own lifecycle (create, update, freeze, unfreeze) and nothing else.

**The freeze rule, as the Timeline implements it (ADR-0028):** a frozen definition's sequence ends the day before its freeze date, the Europe/Rome day of the freeze. An Occurrence due on or after that day does not exist: it never counts toward Monthly Spendable, there is no Backlog, no next due date, nothing a link could pay, and nothing to list or skip — the Occurrences section of a frozen definition is empty and a skip on it is rejected. Occurrences due before the freeze date still count in their months exactly as they did at the time, per Occurrence rather than per month: a weekly cost frozen mid-month keeps its earlier weeks and loses the later ones.

## Considered Options

- **Fix the Budget in place (add a frozen filter to the dashboard)** — rejected: a fifth copy of the rule, and the next rule (ADR-0028's unfreeze, a future "pause") would land in four places again. The bug was a locality problem, not a missing `if`.
- **Put the Timeline in the pure recurrence module** — rejected: `app/recurrence.py` is no-I/O by design and unit-tested directly; loading pins and skips needs the database. The Timeline is the value between the two, and the pure walkers stay underneath it unchanged.
- **One Timeline per kind (a cost Timeline and an income Timeline)** — rejected: the derived reads were already identical after a noun swap; two modules would re-create the drift this ADR removes. ADR-0011's decision stands — two tables, two resources, two screen sides, the lifecycle per side — but its premise that "what the mirror duplicates is a thin layer of guards and CRUD" stopped being true after ADR-0016, ADR-0026 and ADR-0028 landed twice, and ADR-0011 itself said to extract the shared service then.
- **Read "excludes the definition from months whose first post-freeze occurrence would have fallen on or after the freeze date" as a per-month cut** (drop the whole month of the freeze) — rejected: it would retroactively change the Occurrences due earlier in that month, exactly what ADR-0028 rejected ("past Budget numbers would retroactively change"). The per-Occurrence cut is the reading consistent with "months before the freeze date are unaffected".
- **Keep listing a frozen definition's Occurrences, read-only** — rejected: ADR-0028 says the freeze removes its Unpaid and Skipped Occurrences, and a row that can neither be paid nor skipped has no reason to exist.

## Consequences

- `GET /dashboard/budget` stops counting a frozen definition's Occurrences from its freeze date; earlier months are unchanged.
- `GET /recurring-{costs,incomes}/{id}/occurrences` on a frozen definition returns `[]`; `PUT …/occurrences/{date}` on one answers 422. The frontend already renders frozen forms read-only without the Occurrences section, so nothing visible changes there.
- The Recurring list endpoints run two queries per definition instead of five; their output is unchanged.
- Every derived-Occurrence rule change now lands in one module. The HTTP tests on both Recurring resources, the link tests, the skip tests and `test_recurrence.py` survived the move unchanged, which is the evidence the interface did not.
- The remaining mirror — routers, schemas, create/update/freeze/unfreeze, the frontend forms and screens — is still duplicated per kind; collapsing it around the Timeline is a separate decision.
